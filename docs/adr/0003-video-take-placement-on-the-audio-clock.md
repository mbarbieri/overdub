# Video Takes are placed on the audio clock by heard time, and follow the audio transport

The audio interface's output is the only clock. Every duplex callback yields an anchor `(song sample at buffer start, host time)`. JUCE exposes only the output `mHostTime` in duplex, and that is enough. A Video Take is captured through `AVCaptureSession` (camera only) into `AVAssetWriter`. Its first frame's PTS is converted to host time with `CMSyncConvertTime`, then mapped to the song sample that is **heard** at that instant: output IO time + output latency, through a least-squares fit of the take's anchors. That is the same reference Takes use (ADR 0002), so a sound and the frame that shows it land on the same song sample. The Video Take stores that start position in song samples, plus the take's audio-clock rate against host time (the drift ratio). Camera latency isn't reported by any API, so a per-camera **calibration offset** is subtracted; it defaults to 0.

Playback pulls frames: on each display refresh, the heard song position (latest anchor + elapsed host time − output latency + one refresh) is converted to movie time, and the matching decoded frame is shown. The video never runs a clock of its own. Export builds an `AVMutableComposition`, with the offline-rendered mix at 0 and the Video Take at its sample-exact offset, scaled by the drift ratio. `AVAssetExportSession` writes the MP4. The full-song variant uses an `AVMutableVideoComposition` with a black background over the gaps.

The spike (issue #7, branch `spike/av-sync`) measured this on the built-in FaceTime camera with the built-in mic and speakers over an 11.3-minute take. Clap offsets were +0.6 ms at the start and +2.0 ms at the end (sd ≈ 10 ms, against a ±17 ms frame-resolution limit at 29 fps), with no measurable drift. Without the fit, the audio clock's −8.8 ppm would have shifted the video 6 ms by the end. The trimmed export reproduced every clap within 0.8 ms. Playback error was pure frame hold, in [−35, 0] ms.

## Considered Options

- **Wall-clock seconds from the first audio anchor (no fit)**: simplest, but it accumulates the interface's drift against host time. That's only 6 ms over 11 minutes here, but a real interface on its own crystal can be worse, and the fit costs nothing.
- **AVPlayer or AVSampleBufferDisplayLayer slaved to a `CMAudioDeviceClock` (P1/P2)**: less code for continuous play, but it is a second clock to keep in step with the engine's transport on every start, seek and scrub.
- **Camera audio as a scratch track, cross-correlated against the Take (option C)**: automatic per-take offset verification, but it opens the input a second time and was left untested.
- **`AVAssetReader` → `AVAssetWriter` re-stamping export (E2)**: full control, more code. E1 already reproduces offsets to under 1 ms.

## Consequences

- The Song stores a Video Take as: movie file, start position in song samples, drift ratio (song samples per movie second, or ppm), and the camera ID with the calibration value applied at record time.
- The camera calibration also absorbs any error in reported output latency. It is therefore a per-machine setting keyed by camera *and* output device, like ADR 0002's offset. It is never part of a Song. The built-in camera with the built-in devices measured 0 ms.
- Cameras can stall for over a second without a drop callback, so nothing may rebuild timestamps from frame counts. Playback holds the last frame across a gap.
- The full-song export re-times frames onto its output frame grid. That costs up to ±½ frame per frame, but adds no offset.
- Untested, and needed before the spec is final: a USB or Continuity Camera's calibration and a real interface's drift.

## Amendment: the engine renders ahead by the plan latency (issue #24)

The engine's graph delays every Track path by the plan latency L (PDC, ADR 0001), so the transport renders L samples ahead: on each callback the Track nodes read from `position + L`, and after the graph's delay, output sample n of the block is Song sample `position + n`. The anchor's song sample is therefore the Song sample at the block's first **output** sample, after PDC. We chose this so that every consumer (Recording, Video, the playhead) maps an output sample to a Song sample with no latency term of its own, and a plan swap that changes L moves nothing in time.

- Song samples `start .. start + L − 1` are never heard after playback starts at `start` (play, seek, or the first plan). Those output samples are silence: after a seek, play, stop or plan swap, the engine silences the first L output samples, because the graph's delay lines still hold audio rendered before the change. Hearing a few milliseconds less after a start is accepted; outputting stale audio is not.
- The anchor's host time is the device's output IO time for that buffer and **excludes the device's output latency**. Heard time stays output IO time + output latency (+ the calibration offset, ADR 0002), applied by the consumer, as above.
- `Engine::position()` and the anchor both count in output samples: they do not move by L when the plan changes.

## Amendment: camera capture and placement as built (issue #35)

- **Placement.** The fit is over the Pass's anchors only; the first frame's host time maps to the Song sample heard then (fit inverse minus the output latency in samples), minus the camera calibration, rounded to the nearest sample. The drift ratio is the fit's samples per host second divided by the capture clock's rate against host time (`CMSyncGetRelativeRate`, 1 when the session's clock is the host clock).
- **No loopback offset.** The ADR 0002 loopback calibration corrects the whole round trip (input + output) and cannot be split into its output half, so Video Take placement uses the reported output latency only; any output-latency error is absorbed by the camera calibration, which is keyed by the output device for that reason.
- **Movie time 0** is the PTS of the frame that started the writer's session, even when the writer refused that frame; its host time is the one placed.
- **Calibration** is stored in milliseconds per camera ID and output device name (`camera-calibration.json`), converted to samples at the Song's rate when a Video Take is placed; the Video Take keeps the samples applied.
- **The movie runs from the Pass's start to its finish**, pre-roll included, so it may hold frames from before the record point. They are placed by heard time like every other frame (the fit extrapolates a few seconds backwards, well under a sample at observed drift).
- **Failure keeps the audio.** A movie that cannot begin or finish, or a Pass without anchors, adds the Pass without a Video Take; the user sees an Alert (a Video Take was lost), dropped frames a Notice.
- **Events.** The camera code lives in `src/capture`, so ADR 0006's `video.*` start, stop and dropped-frame events are `capture.video_started` and `capture.video_stopped` (with `frames`, `dropped_frames`, `longest_gap_seconds`, `start_sample`, `drift_ppm`), plus `capture.camera_opened` / `capture.camera_closed`. The `video` area stays for playback (slice 15).
- **Live feed.** Record mode's stage shows the camera's preview layer whenever the camera is Armed, recording or not.
- **Clap selftest.** `overdub --selftest clap <package>` takes the Song package; the measurement (`capture/ClapMeasurement.h`) estimates the visual clap as the midpoint of the last two frames of the hands' motion burst, which is good to ±½ frame.

## Amendment: playback as built (issue #36)

- **Heard position.** On each display refresh (`juce::VBlankAttachment` on the main window; one refresh is the measured interval between vertical blanks, 60 Hz until two are seen) the heard Song sample is the latest anchor's Song sample + (now − its host time + one refresh) × the Song's rate − the reported output latency, at the nominal rate (no fit: over one callback the drift is far below a sample). While the transport is stopped, or before the first anchor, it is the engine's position, so a seek or a scrub shows its frame at once (`video/FrameSelection.h`). If no anchor arrives for 250 ms (the audio stalls) the extrapolation stops there, so the picture stops with the sound.
- **Frame selection** is pure: the Video Take covering the heard sample, from its start to the end of its movie (the last frame taken as long as the mean one, through the drift ratio), the one whose Pass is newest in the Song's Pass order where several do; the movie time is (sample − start) / drift ratio; the frame is the last one at or before it, so a stall holds the frame before the gap. No Video Take: black with "No Video Take at the playhead".
- **The movie's length is read from the movie**, not stored in the Song: the frame times are read once per Video Take without decoding (compressed samples) and give the Camera lane's blocks. A Video Take whose movie cannot be read (`video.movie_unreadable`, logged once) covers nothing and shows as a narrow block; one whose movie cannot be opened or decoded for playback (`video.frame_unavailable`, logged once) shows black and is not tried again until the Song is reopened.
- **Decoding** (`video/MovieFile.h`): one `AVAssetReader` per shown Video Take, read forward; a step back or a jump of more than 30 frames restarts it at the frame. The decoded `CMSampleBuffer` is enqueued on an `AVSampleBufferDisplayLayer` (its `sampleBufferRenderer`) marked to display immediately: the layer's own timebase is never used.
- **Where it shows.** Edit mode's floating Video Preview window (the toolbar's Video toggle, off by default; it floats in Edit mode only), and Record mode's stage when it does not show the camera's live feed. The stage shows the live feed while the camera is Armed and a Pass runs or the transport is stopped (to frame the shot), playback otherwise; this narrows issue #35's "whenever the camera is Armed".

## Amendment: MP4 export as built (issue #38)

- **Composition.** `exporting::exportMovie` (`export/MovieExport.h`) renders the mix (the same offline render as WAV/M4A) into a hidden WAV beside the destination, then composes it at time 0 with the Video Take's movie inserted at its start in Song samples (a `CMTime` with the Song rate as timescale) and scaled by the drift ratio (`scaleTimeRange`). `AVAssetExportSession` (highest-quality preset) writes H.264 + AAC MP4 into a hidden partial file renamed over the destination when complete; the mix file is always deleted. Progress is the mix render's first half, the encode's second.
- **Range** (`export/MovieSpan.h`, pure): trimmed is the Video Take's span; full song is [0, max (mix end incl. tails, Video Take end)). Nothing before Song sample 0 is exported. The Video Take's end is its start + the movie's duration (`video::readMovieTiming`, the last frame as long as the mean one) through the drift ratio, the same value the Camera lane and the Export page use (`video::videoTakeEnd`). The mix is padded with silence when a Video Take outlasts it (written into the mix WAV, see below).
- **Full-song re-timing.** The output frame grid is the Video Take's mean frame duration on the Song clock (so a steady camera keeps a constant phase, and the offset does not wander from the first frame to the last), and the movie enters the composition half a grid step early, so each grid point shows the nearest source frame: up to ±½ frame per frame, and no systematic offset (each placement's offset is its constant phase on the grid, within ±½ frame, zero on average over placements). Before the movie the background is black; after it, the export session would end the video track where the movie ends, so the movie's first frame is stretched over the remainder at opacity 0 and the black background shows.
- **Measured** by the round trip (`tests/export/MovieExportTest.cpp`: a 10 s 30 fps movie with flash frames, placed ~1 s in with a 500 ppm drift ratio, inside a 12 s mix with a click at each flash's Song sample): trimmed, every flash within 0.25 ms of its click and end − start 0.5 ms; full song, a constant −15.7 ms (that placement's phase on the grid, inside ±½ frame) with end − start 0.
- **A Song with no Clips** (issue #67) exports an MP4 of its Video Take over a silent mix. The mix WAV is always padded with silence to the range's end (`exporting::writeMixFile`), so the MP4's AAC track is as long as its video whether the Video Take outlasts the mix or there is no mix at all. The range follows the rule above with a mix end of 0 (no Clips and no Insert Chain tail): trimmed is the Video Take's span, full song is [0, Video Take end). Only an empty range (a Video Take that ends before Song sample 0) is `export.nothing_to_export` for MP4. WAV and M4A of a Song with no Clips and no tail stay refused with `export.nothing_to_export`, shown as the Notice "The Song has no Clips to export.": an audio file of nothing but silence is not a mix.
- **Choice.** The Export page shows the Song's stored `exportVideoTakeId` (else the first Video Take); picking one stores it outside history. Errors: `ui.no_video_take` (Notice, raised by `ui::OpenSong`), `export.video_take_unreadable`, `export.movie_failed` (Alerts).

## Amendment: the camera calibration flow as built (issue #39)

- Settings' calibration panel shows the camera calibration stored for the Song's chosen camera and the current output device ("Not calibrated: 0 ms" by default) and how to record a clap Pass (camera Armed, 3 to 5 claps in frame about a second apart).
- **Measure Last Clap Pass** runs the clap selftest's measurement on the open Song's newest Video Take (`capture::measureNewestVideoTake`, shared with `overdub --selftest clap`). When a clap is seen it offers the calibration that Video Take was placed with (its `calibrationOffsetSamples` in ms) plus the mean clap offset, stored for **that Video Take's camera** and the current output device: the Video Take does not record its output device, so the flow assumes the clap Pass was recorded on the device open now.
- Video Takes already recorded keep their placement; the next Pass uses the new value.
